ABOUT

About the Research

A student research project at the University of Portland exploring how computational modeling can help make transcranial magnetic stimulation more efficient and accessible.

OUR FOCUS

Understanding TMS from the brain region down to the neuron

Transcranial magnetic stimulation uses rapidly changing magnetic fields to induce electric fields in the brain. In depression treatment, stimulation is commonly aimed at the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), while the motor cortex is used to determine a patient-specific motor threshold.

Our work uses computational models to study how stimulation settings interact with cortical anatomy and neuronal morphology. A major focus of this website is comparing modeled responses in the DLPFC and motor cortex and examining how coil orientation, pulse width, pulse shape, cortical layer, and neuron geometry affect firing threshold.

WHY IT MATTERS

Steps toward more accessible TMS

Current TMS systems are large, power intensive, and typically used in specialized clinical settings. By identifying stimulation conditions that reduce the neuronal firing threshold in simulation, this research investigates design directions that may support more efficient future TMS systems.

HOW WE STUDY IT

Multi-scale computational modeling

The project combines electric-field modeling and detailed neuronal simulations. SimNIBS is used to model induced electric fields, while NeMo-TMS and NEURON are used to examine how morphologically realistic cortical neurons respond to stimulation.

THIS WEBSITE

An interactive research companion

The site brings together the project's background, brain-region analysis, neuron morphology results, partner work, and research outputs. The interactive 3D brain is designed to connect the engineering results to the anatomy involved in TMS treatment.

Part of a broader collaboration: This University of Portland work is one component of a multi-institutional research effort that also includes collaborators at Baylor University and UC San Diego.
